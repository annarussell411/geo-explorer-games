Evolution in Action on the Galápagos

The Story: Over 4 million years ago, finches from South America landed on the Galápagos islands. With no competition, they spread across the islands and adapted to different foods. One ancestor became 18 different species—each with a beak shaped perfectly for what it eats. That's evolution in real time! 🌋

🧠 The Big Idea: Adaptation

Darwin's finches show us that when creatures face different challenges, they change over many generations. On islands with hard seeds, birds with thick, strong beaks survived. On islands with delicate flowers, birds with long, thin beaks thrived. Nature doesn't change a creature on purpose—but the creatures best suited to their island survived and had babies. That's natural selection, and it's how one finch ancestor became 18 different species!

🧛‍♂️ Vampire Finch: One finch species is so weird it drinks blood from other birds. It uses its pointy beak to peck at booby birds and sip their blood. Evolution is wild!
